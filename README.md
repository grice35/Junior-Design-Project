# ECE-3011-Project

The Mario Kart Racer is a fun, Mario Kart-themed racing robot designed to get kids excited about engineering. Science kits are often not affordable or intriguing enough for a classroom. This project solves that by creating a car that is affordable, easy to understand, and uses lights and music to show how it works. By using a theme familiar to kids aged 8-10, we make a complex subject like engineering simpler and more fascinating to learn for them.
The Mario Kart Racer is designed to autonomously follow a black line on light gray foam tiles. To make it a great teaching tool, the inner workings of the robot is visible so kids can see the circuit board and other electronics, like sensors and motors. In addition to autonomously driving on the black line, the Mario Kart Racer interacts with its environment in the form of LED lights and music that changes based on how the car moves. The robot will also be able to be remotely controlled to allow students to learn about wireless technology.
The robot uses photoresistors to see the difference between the black line and the lighter tile color. We will use an ESP32 microcontroller to program the motors to respond correctly based on sensor inputs, as well as include audio and visual cues to enable the audience to understand what the Mario Kart Racer is doing.
The proposed Mario Kart Racer will cost less than $100 to build, ensuring affordability for all families with kids under 10 years old and educational institutions looking to expose students to more complex engineering and STEAM concepts. Further, the Mario Kart Racer will be small, lightweight, and safe enough for students to carry and play with. The user interface will be simple to allow anyone with the smallest amount of knowledge in robotics to use and demonstrate.
